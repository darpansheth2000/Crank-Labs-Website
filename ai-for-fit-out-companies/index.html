AI for fit-out companies

Fit-out company software that joins the office to the site

Fit-out contractors rarely lose money on the work they price. They lose it on versions, variations and decisions that live in inboxes and WhatsApp groups. We build one live project record, from enquiry to final account, around the tools you already use.

In short

Fit-out company software should hold one live record per project, from enquiry to final account, and use AI where it saves real hours: drafting BOQs from drawings and past jobs for an estimator to approve, turning site photos into client progress reports, flagging site changes never billed as variations, and chasing material decisions before they delay the programme. It should connect to your Excel, accounting and construction apps, starting with one workflow live in weeks.

What should fit-out company software actually do?

Good fit-out company software is not one more app for the site team to ignore. It is a single live record per project, running from the first enquiry to the last retention payment, with AI doing the reading and drafting: turning drawings and past BOQs into a draft estimate, turning site photos and voice notes into a client progress report, and spotting work agreed on site that was never priced as a variation. An estimator, project manager or finance lead approves anything that carries money or a commitment. The rest is well-built software and automation, joined to the spreadsheets, accounting system and construction apps you already run.

This page is for owners and operations heads of fit-out and interior contractors. Crank Labs works with fit-out and interior contractors in the UAE and with kitchen and interior manufacturers in India, so what follows reflects how those businesses actually run, described generically.

Why do fit-out projects leak margin between the office and the site?

The same patterns appear once a business runs more projects than the owner can personally watch:

  • Version chaos in quotations. Revision four went to the client, revision six is on the estimator's laptop, and site is building from revision three.
  • Variations not billed. The designer asks for an extra bulkhead on a site walk, the foreman agrees, and it never reaches an invoice.
  • Site updates scattered across WhatsApp groups. Several groups per project, and the photo you need for a dispute is lost a month later.
  • Late material decisions holding up the programme. A finish waits on approval, the long-lead item is ordered late, and handover slides.
  • Collections trail progress. The milestone passed a fortnight ago, but the payment application waits for evidence.
  • Margin is visible only at the end. By the final account, any overrun is history.

One project record that office and site both write to, with automation and AI on top, fixes most of it.

How should enquiries and site surveys be handled?

Enquiries arrive from tenants, designers and project managers, often on WhatsApp with a floor plan and a vague brief. An AI agent on WhatsApp and email replies in seconds, asks what your sales lead would ask (area, use, opening date, budget range), collects drawings, opens the project record and books the survey.

On site, the surveyor fills a phone form with dimensions, photos and a voice note, which AI turns into a written summary ready for estimating.

How does BOQ and estimation automation work without losing control?

Estimating is where jobs are won or lost, and where expensive people spend hours retyping. BOQ and estimation automation leaves the estimator with judgement, not data entry.

  1. Read the tender pack. AI reads drawings, specifications and the finishes schedule, lists items and quantities, and marks anything it is unsure of.
  2. Match to your own history. Items are matched to your rate library and similar past BOQs, so the draft uses your rates and allowances.
  3. Flag the gaps. Unmatched items, missing specifications and likely exclusions are listed for the estimator, not guessed.
  4. The estimator approves. Nothing priced leaves the business until an estimator has checked quantities, rates and margin.
  5. Version everything. Each revision records what changed and who approved it, so client, office and site see the same version.

Drawing quality varies, so treat AI extraction as a fast first draft. The estimator owns the number.

How do you stop variations going unbilled?

Most unbilled variations are changes agreed in a corridor and forgotten. The fix is to make raising a variation quicker than not raising one:

  • The site engineer raises it from the phone in a minute: what changed, where, who instructed it, with photos.
  • The estimator prices it from the same rate library as the BOQ, and the project manager sends it for client approval.
  • Approved variations flow into the next payment application, while pending ones stay visible on the dashboard.
  • AI reads daily site notes and photos and flags work that looks like extra scope with no matching variation, for a person to review.

How can clients approve materials and finishes faster?

Late finish decisions are a common reason fit-out programmes slip. Two things help. An approvals register gives every material and finish a decision-by date worked back from the programme, with client reminders before it becomes critical. And AI image tools can show the client the same space in two or three finishes, so the decision happens in one meeting rather than three. Renders are indicative; the approved physical sample and signed submittal remain the reference.

Once a finish is approved, its order-by date is fixed and procurement sees it. Long-lead items such as joinery, imported stone and specialist lighting are flagged early. If you run your own joinery workshop, our page on AI for manufacturing companies covers the production side.

How do daily site updates become client reports?

Site teams already take photos all day; they just vanish into chat groups. Construction project tracking works when capture is as quick as a WhatsApp message:

  • The supervisor picks the project and area on a simple phone screen, takes photos and records a short voice note.
  • Each photo is saved by project, area and date, so evidence for a claim is one search away.
  • AI drafts the progress report: work done by area, issues, deliveries awaited and client decisions needed.
  • The project manager edits and approves, and the report goes to the client on an agreed day.

Subcontractors get a weekly look-ahead they acknowledge. WhatsApp can stay the channel; the record lives in one place.

What about snagging, handover and getting paid?

Snagging and handover

Snags are logged as photos against a room, assigned to a trade and closed with a photo of the fix. The handover pack of as-builts, warranties, manuals and certificates builds up as the job runs.

Payment milestones and collections

When a milestone is reached, the payment application is drafted with evidence and approved variations attached. Finance approves it, reminders follow an agreed schedule, and workflow automation posts the invoice into Zoho Books or TallyPrime once a person signs off. Retention release dates are tracked so they are claimed on time.

What should an owner dashboard show?

  • Margin per project: contract value plus approved variations, against committed and actual cost.
  • Variations raised, priced, approved, pending and not yet billed.
  • Client decisions overdue and long-lead items at risk.
  • Progress against programme, taken from the site record.
  • Payment applications due, submitted and overdue, and retention outstanding.
  • A plain-language weekly summary written by AI from the same records.

Do we have to replace Excel or our construction management app?

No. Interior fit-out project management software works best as a layer over what you already have:

  • Excel stays where it is useful. Estimators keep their BOQ templates, and the system reads and writes the same format.
  • Accounting stays the system of record. Invoices, payments and costs stay in Zoho Books or TallyPrime, and Xero or QuickBooks Online are tools with an API we can connect to.
  • Construction management apps with an API feed the dashboard and receive updates, so nothing is entered twice.
  • One owner per field. Each piece of data belongs to one system, so the numbers never disagree.

What does this look like for a 60-person Dubai fit-out contractor?

Consider a generic fit-out company in Dubai with around 60 people running 8 to 12 office and retail projects at once, with three estimators and five project managers. Quotes live on estimators' laptops, every project has several WhatsApp groups, and the owner learns about margin at final account.

A typical phased build starts on site: supervisors capture photos and voice notes per area, and project managers approve a weekly client report drafted from them. Next, quotations move into one versioned record, and site engineers raise variations from the phone, each priced by an estimator and tracked to the payment application. An approvals register then gives every finish a decision-by date with client reminders. Accounting stays in Zoho Books, and the owner sees margin, variations and collections per project on one screen.

What guardrails should AI for interior contractors have?

  • An estimator approves every BOQ and quotation; a project manager approves every client report and variation sent.
  • Finance approves before any payment application, invoice or accounting entry goes out.
  • Authority, landlord and building approvals and permits stay with your team; the system only tracks their status and dates.
  • Renders are labelled indicative, and approved samples remain the reference.
  • Messages the business starts on WhatsApp use approved templates and the client's opt-in, as the WhatsApp Business Platform requires.

What should you ask a partner, and what are the red flags?

Questions to ask

  • How does it handle a quote revised five times, and a variation raised on site?
  • Will you design with our estimators and supervisors, not only management?
  • How does it connect to our Excel, accounting and construction apps?
  • Does site capture work on a basic phone with patchy signal?
  • What goes live first, and how will we measure whether it worked?

Red flags

  • A proposal that starts by replacing every tool you use.
  • AI estimates sent to clients with no estimator approval.
  • A demo built on someone else's BOQs, with no plan for your rate library.
  • Unofficial WhatsApp tools instead of the WhatsApp Business Platform.
  • A fixed price before anyone has seen how a project moves through your business.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI produce a BOQ directly from our drawings?

It can produce a fast first draft. AI reads drawings, specifications and finishes schedules, lists items and quantities, and matches them to your rate library and similar past BOQs. Drawing quality varies, so it marks items it is unsure of. An estimator checks quantities, rates and margin before anything is priced to a client, which keeps the commercial judgement with your team while removing most of the retyping.

Will our site supervisors actually use a new app?

Only if it is quicker than what they do now. Site capture should feel like sending a WhatsApp message: pick the project and area, take photos, record a voice note. No long forms, no typing on a dusty site. Designing the screens with supervisors, running them on one or two projects first and fixing what they find awkward is what earns adoption across the rest.

Is this worth it for a smaller fit-out company with 20 or 30 people?

Often yes, if you start with the workflow that costs the most time or money, such as unbilled variations, site reporting or enquiry response. A smaller team usually has the same few people quoting, running sites and chasing payments, so removing retyping and chasing frees real hours. Start with one focused build connected to the tools you already use, not a company-wide programme.

Can it work with the construction management software we already use?

Usually, if that software has an API. The new layer reads projects, tasks or progress from it and writes updates back, so the site team never enters anything twice. Where a tool has no API, a scheduled export or a small connector often works. Each piece of data is given one owning system so the numbers on the dashboard always match the source.

Where is our project and client data stored?

Systems run on infrastructure Crank Labs controls or on your own cloud, with access limited to what each workflow needs and actions logged. A person approves before anything financial is sent or posted, such as payment applications or invoices into Zoho Books or TallyPrime. Consent and retention for client data are planned with the UAE Personal Data Protection Law, Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, in mind.

How long before we see results?

A focused first workflow, such as a WhatsApp enquiry agent or the site photo and progress report flow, is typically live in two to four weeks. Larger builds that connect quotations, variations, accounting and the owner dashboard are phased, so the first part goes live early and results show from the first month while the rest is built and connected.

Can AI handle authority approvals and permits for our projects?

No, and it should not. Authority, landlord and building management approvals and permits carry legal and safety responsibility, so they stay with your own team and consultants. What software can do is track each approval's status, owner and due date against the programme, store the submitted documents with the project, and remind the right person before a delay hits the site.
