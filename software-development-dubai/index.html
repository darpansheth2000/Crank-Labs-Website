How does a custom software project run?

The expensive mistakes happen before any code is written, when a team builds exactly what was asked for and the request turns out to be a workaround for a broken process. So every engagement follows the same three stages.

  1. Diagnose. A free working session mapping how leads, operations and finance flow today, who touches each step and where time and money leak. The output is a ranked shortlist of candidate builds.
  2. Redesign and build. Agree the target process with the people who will use it, then build it on real data. The first release goes live in weeks, not quarters, and covers one workflow for one team.
  3. Run and scale. Measure the result against the old way, fix what users trip over, train the team, then move to the next function once the first change pays for itself.

Phased releases matter because people only say what they really need once they are using something. This is the thinking behind full stack consulting: the people who diagnose the problem are the people who build and roll out the fix.

Who owns the code and data, and where is it hosted?

Settle ownership in writing before any build starts, whoever you hire. What to agree:

  • Data. Your business data stays yours and can be exported in a standard format whenever you ask.
  • Code. Ownership of the source code and your right to a full copy are set out in the contract, not left to goodwill.
  • Hosting. The system runs on your own cloud account or on infrastructure Crank Labs controls. Both are sensible; it depends on who you want managing it.
  • Accounts. Domains, cloud subscriptions and third-party services sit in your company's name, never a developer's personal account.
  • Access. Each user and integration reaches only the data its workflow needs, and access can be removed quickly when someone leaves.

The UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) sets rules on how businesses collect and process personal data, including having a lawful basis, limiting data to its purpose and respecting people's rights over their information. Firms registered in the DIFC and ADGM fall under those free zones' own data protection regimes, and healthcare providers carry extra obligations. We design for this from the first release, with role-based access and activity logs. That is engineering practice, not legal advice, so confirm your obligations with your legal adviser.

What does a fixed-scope first phase look like?

A first phase should be small enough to ship in weeks and useful enough that people choose to use it. It is agreed in writing and usually covers:

  • One workflow, for example job intake to quote, or document collection for one service line.
  • One team of users, with roles and permissions defined.
  • One or two integrations, usually accounting plus the CRM or WhatsApp.
  • Migration of the live data that workflow needs, not every historical record.
  • A measure of success agreed upfront, such as hours of retyping removed or status calls avoided.
  • Training for the users and a period of close support straight after launch.

What should you ask a software development company in Dubai?

  • Who will we speak to each week, and are they the people building the software?
  • What will be live and in use by the end of the first month?
  • How will you connect to the tools we already use instead of replacing them?
  • Who owns the code, the data and the hosting accounts?
  • How do you handle personal data under the UAE PDPL?
  • Who fixes problems after launch, and how quickly?

Red flags: a fixed price for a large system before anyone has mapped the process, no working software until the final month, a proposal to replace tools that work fine, vague answers on ownership, and no clear answer on where your data will be stored.

A worked example: a Dubai fit-out contractor

A 60-person fit-out contractor in Dubai typically runs on a patchwork: enquiries on WhatsApp, quotes in Excel, drawings in email threads, site progress in a group chat and invoicing in Zoho Books. Variations get lost between the site and the office.

A sensible sequence for that business:

  1. First phase. A project tracker for one delivery team, with each job's stage, site photos and a variation log that needs sign-off before extra work starts.
  2. Second phase. A client portal on the same data, so clients see progress and approve variations themselves, with WhatsApp notifications to those who have opted in.
  3. Third phase. Milestones that create draft invoices in Zoho Books for the accounts team to approve, and an owner dashboard showing margin and collections by project.

Each phase is useful on its own and measured before the next one starts. The contractor keeps Zoho Books and the habits that already work. When the software becomes part of a wider change in how the whole firm runs, that is digital transformation in Dubai, and the rollout matters as much as the code.

Frequently asked questions

Do you build native mobile apps?

No. Crank Labs builds full stack web apps that work well on phones, tablets and laptops. For staff tools and client portals that is usually the better choice: there is no app store approval, every user is always on the latest version, and one codebase serves every device. A site supervisor updating a job or a client checking progress can do it from the browser on their phone.

Will custom software replace Zoho Books or TallyPrime?

Usually not. Accounting is a solved problem, so we keep Zoho Books or TallyPrime as the system of record for finance and build around it. The custom software handles the work that happens before and after the accounts: jobs, approvals, documents and client updates. Data flows into accounting as drafts that your accounts team approves before anything is posted.

Can you take over software another developer built?

Sometimes. During the free audit we review what exists, who owns the code and accounts, and how well it is documented. Then we give you a straight recommendation: extend it, stabilise it, or replace specific parts. If the source code or hosting accounts sit with the previous developer, recovering them comes first, because nobody can safely change a system they cannot fully access.

Can the software be hosted in the UAE or on our own cloud?

Yes. We can run the system on your own cloud account, in the region your IT or legal adviser prefers, or on infrastructure Crank Labs controls. Running it on your account gives you direct control of the data and billing. Running it on ours means we handle hosting, monitoring and updates as part of the monthly managed plan. Either way, access is limited to what each workflow needs.

Do we need an in-house IT team to run custom software?

No. Many of the firms we work with have one person who looks after systems alongside another role, or nobody at all. The monthly managed plan covers hosting, monitoring, fixes and small improvements. If you do have an IT team, we agree who owns which part, share access through proper accounts rather than shared passwords, and document what we build.

Do you work with businesses outside Dubai?

Yes. Our UAE office is in Dubai, and we work with businesses across the emirates, including Abu Dhabi and Sharjah, with on-site visits for working sessions and rollout where they help. Much of the build and support is remote. Multi-branch firms are common, so we design for routing, permissions and reporting by branch from the first release.

What happens after the software goes live?

The monthly managed plan takes over. We monitor the system, fix issues, tune what users find awkward and make small improvements as the business changes. We also measure the result against the agreed success measure from the first phase. Once that first change is paying for itself, we scope the next phase with you, whether that is another workflow, another team or another integration.
