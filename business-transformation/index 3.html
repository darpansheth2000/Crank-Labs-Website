What is business transformation consulting?

Business transformation consulting is outside help to change how the core of a business runs: how leads become sales, how orders become deliveries, and how deliveries become cash. Good business transformation consulting does not stop at a redesigned process map. Crank Labs redesigns how sales, operations and finance work, builds the software, AI agents and automation the new process needs, and leads the rollout with your team until the new way is simply how work gets done.

For a business of 20 to 500 people, transformation rarely means a big-bang programme. It means fixing the flows that leak time and money, one at a time, in an order that lets each change fund the next.

Business transformation or digital transformation consulting?

Digital transformation consulting usually means putting new technology in: a new ERP, a CRM, a cloud migration. Business transformation asks a prior question: should this process exist in this form at all? Buying software for a broken process gives you a broken process with a subscription fee. We treat technology as the means, and a measurable change in speed, cost or revenue as the point. The distinction is set out in more detail in digital transformation vs business transformation.

What should you transform first?

Our view: start with the flow closest to cash that has a clear owner and can be measured within a month. That is usually lead response in sales or invoice processing in finance. It is almost never an ERP replacement.

CandidateStart here ifWait if

Operations: order to deliveryOrders are re-keyed between sales, production and dispatch, and status questions eat staff timeThe product itself is still changing every month

A quick test: if you cannot name the person who will own the new process and the number you will check every week, it is not ready to go first.

What does process redesign look like in practice?

A 120-person kitchen and interior manufacturer typically runs on a chain of handoffs. Enquiries come in through WhatsApp and showroom visits. A designer measures on site and sends photos to a personal phone. Quotes are built in a spreadsheet and revised several times over email. Once an order is confirmed, production is planned in another spreadsheet, installation is scheduled by phone, and finance chases milestone payments by checking with the project manager.

Process redesign for that business usually involves five changes, built in this order:

  1. One record per job, created at the first enquiry and carried through to final payment, so nobody re-keys details.
  2. An AI agent on WhatsApp that qualifies the enquiry and books the site visit.
  3. A quoting tool built on the current price and material list, so revisions take minutes rather than days.
  4. Production and installation status updated from the shop floor and visible to sales, so customers get answers without three phone calls.
  5. Milestone invoices drafted automatically when a stage is marked complete, and posted to accounts after approval.

Each is a separate build, sequenced by value. The custom parts are full stack software development where off-the-shelf tools do not fit, and AI automation where the work is repetitive.

How do you measure a business transformation?

Measure before you build. If you do not record today's baseline, the review becomes an argument about whether anything changed. Pick two or three measures per function, no more.

FunctionMeasuresHow to get the baseline
SalesTime to first reply, enquiries with no follow-up, enquiries that become a booking or orderExport a month of WhatsApp and email enquiries and check them by hand
OperationsOrder cycle time, orders re-keyed, status queries per weekTrace a sample of recent orders from confirmation to delivery
FinanceTime from delivery to invoice, entry time per invoice, days to close the monthTime a week of entries and look at the last few month-ends
LeadershipHow long until numbers are available, how often they are disputedNote when last month's report actually arrived

Alongside the numbers, track adoption. Are people using the new system, or quietly going back to the old spreadsheet? Falling adoption is the earliest warning sign of a change that will not stick.

Change management: how do you bring the team with you?

Most operational transformation fails on people, not technology. What works:

  • Map with the people who do the work. The accounts assistant knows why invoices get stuck. The manager often does not.
  • Name one owner per process. A person inside the business who decides, not a committee.
  • Train on real cases. Use this week's invoices and enquiries, not a demo.
  • Run old and new side by side, briefly. A short overlap builds trust. A long one means people never switch.
  • Remove the old path. If the old spreadsheet still works, it will still be used.
  • Be clear about roles. Tell people what the system takes off their plate and what they will do instead. Silence breeds resistance.
  • Review weekly at first. Fix small annoyances fast, because they become the reason people give up.

Why do business transformation projects stall?

The patterns repeat. Watch for these red flags before and during any programme:

  • The roadmap lists twenty initiatives and no first build.
  • The sponsor delegates the programme and stops attending reviews.
  • Success is defined as "system implemented" rather than a change in speed, cost or revenue.
  • The consultants leave at go-live, before anyone has used the system under pressure.
  • The plan starts by replacing every tool at once.
  • Nobody measured the baseline, so nobody can say whether it worked.

Frequently asked questions

How long does a business transformation take?

It depends on scope, but it should not take months before anything changes. A focused change, such as WhatsApp lead response or invoice entry, is typically live in two to four weeks. Larger work across several systems is phased so results show from the first month. A full transformation of sales, operations and finance then proceeds function by function, each one starting once the previous change has paid for itself.

Do we need to replace our ERP or accounting software?

Usually not. Most of the time lost in a growing business sits between systems, in re-keying, chasing and manual reporting, rather than inside them. Crank Labs connects TallyPrime, Zoho Books, CRMs, Microsoft 365, Gmail and WhatsApp so data moves on its own. Replacing a core system is sometimes the right call, but it should follow a redesign that proves what you need, not lead it.

How much does business transformation consulting cost?

Crank Labs charges a one-time build fee for each phase plus a monthly managed plan that covers hosting, monitoring, tuning and support. The exact quote comes after the free audit, because cost depends on how many systems are involved, how clean the data is, and how many teams the rollout touches. Each phase is scoped separately, so you never commit to the whole programme up front.

Who from our team needs to be involved?

Three roles matter. A sponsor, usually the owner or COO, who makes decisions and attends reviews. A process owner for each flow being changed, who knows the work and will run the new process. And the people who do the work day to day, for a few hours during mapping and again during testing and training. Their time is the biggest factor in pace.

Can we transform one department without touching the others?

Yes, and that is often the right start. Finance or sales can be redesigned on its own as long as the handoffs to other teams are mapped. A new invoice flow, for example, needs to know when operations marks a job complete. We design those connection points from the start, so the next function slots in later without rework.

Is business transformation only for large companies?

No. The firms we work with typically have somewhere between 20 and 500 people. At that size the gains from removing re-keying, slow replies and manual reporting are felt directly by the owner, and decisions can be made quickly. What changes with size is the phasing: a smaller business might complete a function in weeks, a larger one plans it across several months.

Mapping sessions and rollout training are often done on site, because watching the work and training on live cases matters. Build, testing and ongoing support are mostly remote, with regular review calls.
