How do you decide where AI is worth it?

Most businesses have more AI ideas than they need. The real work is ruling most of them out. We run every candidate through five tests, in this order:

  1. Volume and repetition. Does the task happen dozens of times a week in roughly the same shape? Reading supplier invoices, answering the same twenty enquiry questions and building the weekly sales report all qualify. A quarterly board pack does not.
  2. Cost of delay. Does waiting cost money? An enquiry answered the next morning is often an enquiry lost. An invoice entered days late slows collections. If nobody suffers when the task waits, it is a weak candidate.
  3. Rules or judgement. Could a capable new hire do the task after a week with a written guide? Then an AI agent or workflow can probably handle most of it, with people taking the exceptions. If it needs years of experience, AI should assist, not decide.
  4. Data available. Is the information the task needs written down somewhere a system can reach? If the answers live only in one manager's head, the first job is writing them down.
  5. Cost of a mistake. What happens if the output is wrong? A clumsy reply is cheap to fix. A wrong payment or a wrong medical instruction is not. High-stakes outputs get a human approval step before anything is sent or posted.

What does a first AI implementation look like?

A 60-person distributor with sales on WhatsApp and finance in Tally typically has the same three leaks. Enquiries land on the personal phones of four or five salespeople, so nobody knows how many came in or how fast they were answered. Quotes are typed from a price list into a spreadsheet. Supplier bills and customer invoices are keyed into Tally by hand, and the owner sees the month's numbers a couple of weeks after it closes.

A sensible plan for that business does not start with a company-wide AI programme. It starts with one flow:

  • Weeks one to four: move enquiries onto one WhatsApp Business Platform number, with an AI agent for WhatsApp enquiries that replies in seconds, asks the qualifying questions a good salesperson would, drafts the quote from the current price list and hands the conversation to the right person.
  • Next: automated invoice and bill entry, where documents are read, matched to the right ledger and posted into Tally after an accounts person approves them.
  • Then: a daily dashboard of enquiries, quotes, orders and receivables, built from systems that now hold clean data.

Each step is measured before the next one starts. If the first change does not pay for itself, there is no reason to fund the second.

How much does AI consulting cost?

We do not publish prices, because the honest answer depends on your systems. Structurally, Crank Labs charges a one-time build fee plus a monthly managed plan that covers hosting, monitoring, tuning and support. You get an exact quote after the free audit, once we have seen what needs connecting.

What moves the cost up or down is predictable:

  • How many systems the build connects to, and whether they offer usable integrations or need workarounds.
  • How many channels (WhatsApp, voice, email, web) and how many languages.
  • How clean the data is today, and how much tidying has to come first.
  • How many approval steps and exception paths the process needs.
  • Volume: how many conversations, documents or transactions pass through each month.

Be wary of paying for a long discovery phase that ends in a report. Discovery should be short and should produce a build plan. For a wider view of what drives budgets, see what AI automation costs a small business.

Questions to ask AI consultants before you sign

  • Which of your past builds are still running today, and who maintains them?
  • Will you build on our existing tools, or ask us to replace them?
  • Where will our data be stored and processed, and who can access it?
  • Which outputs will a person approve before they are sent or posted?
  • What will you measure, and what is the baseline today?
  • Who owns the prompts, workflows and code if we part ways?
  • What happens when the AI is unsure? Show us the handover.

Red flags in an AI strategy consulting proposal

  • Guaranteed savings figures before anyone has looked at your processes.
  • A licence for a platform the consultant resells, presented as the strategy.
  • No mention of data access, approvals or what happens when the system gets it wrong.
  • A plan that replaces your accounting software or CRM before proving anything.
  • Months of workshops before a single working prototype.
  • No named person on their side responsible after go-live.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need clean data before starting an AI project?

No, but you need to know where it lives. Part of the audit is a data readiness check: which systems hold the information, how complete it is and what needs tidying. Many first projects, such as WhatsApp lead response, need very little historical data, just approved answers and a calendar. Others, like automated reporting, need the source data fixed first, and that fix becomes step one of the plan.

Can AI work with Tally, Zoho Books or our existing CRM?

Yes. Crank Labs builds around the tools you already use, including TallyPrime, Zoho Books, Microsoft 365, Gmail, popular CRMs, calendars and custom databases, plus the WhatsApp Business Platform. The aim is to remove re-keying between systems, not to make you migrate. Replacing a core system is rarely the right first move and is never a condition of working with us.

Will AI replace our staff?

In the businesses we work with, AI takes over the repetitive part of a role: typing invoices, answering the same enquiry questions, chasing documents, building reports. People keep the judgement, the exceptions and the relationships. Most owners use the time released to handle more volume with the same team or to put people on work that grows revenue. We plan that conversation with you before rollout.

Which AI models and platforms do you use?

We choose per task rather than tying every client to one provider. What matters more than the model is how the system is built: which data it can reach, what rules it follows, when it hands over to a person and how its actions are logged. Systems run on infrastructure Crank Labs controls or on your own cloud, and you can ask exactly what runs where.

Do you work with businesses outside Dubai and Mumbai?

Yes. Crank Labs has offices in Mumbai and Dubai and delivers across India, the UAE and Australia. Most of the build and support is done remotely, with on-site visits for working sessions and rollout where they help. Time spent on site is planned around the moments that matter, such as mapping the process with the team or training on live work.

What do we need to prepare for the free audit?

Very little. Bring the person who owns the process you want to fix and, if possible, someone who does the work day to day. It helps to have a rough idea of volumes, such as enquiries or invoices a week, and to know which tools you use. We map the flow together during the session and tell you what the first build would be.

What happens after the system goes live?

The monthly managed plan keeps it running: hosting, monitoring, tuning prompts and rules as your business changes, fixing integrations when a connected tool updates, and training new staff. We review results against the baseline with you. Once the first change has paid for itself, we usually move on to the next function, such as finance after sales.
