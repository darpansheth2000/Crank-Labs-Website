How do you work with formal procurement, security reviews and approvals?

Many Abu Dhabi organisations, including larger family groups and healthcare providers, run a structured process before a new vendor can start: forms, documentation, an IT or security review, sometimes a committee sign-off. We treat that as part of the project:

  • Documentation. A written scope, the current and target process maps, a plain description of how data flows through each workflow, and a list of every system the build will read from or write to.
  • Data residency questions. We state where data is stored and processed for each workflow. Where you need data to stay in a particular location or under your control, we can build on your own cloud account in the region you choose.
  • Security reviews. Named accounts rather than shared passwords, access limited to what each workflow needs, actions logged, and access you can remove at any time. We answer your IT team's questionnaire ourselves.
  • Approval steps inside the system. For finance and healthcare, a person approves before anything is sent or posted, and the approval rules can mirror your existing delegation of authority.
  • Realistic scheduling. Diagnose work and documentation move while approvals run.

A worked example: a family-owned services group in Abu Dhabi

Take a family-owned group of around 200 people, with a facilities maintenance company, a trading arm and a small property portfolio, run from Abu Dhabi with a branch in Al Ain. Service requests arrive on several staff mobiles and a shared email inbox. Purchase orders above a certain value need a signature from a family member, which can mean days of waiting when they are travelling. Each company keeps its own books, and the group report is built by hand.

A sensible sequence for that group:

  1. One official WhatsApp number for service requests, with an agent that logs the job, asks for photos and location, and assigns it to the right team in Abu Dhabi or Al Ain. Typically live in two to four weeks.
  2. Purchase order approvals sent to the right approver by WhatsApp or email with the supporting documents attached, approved or rejected in one tap, with every decision logged.
  3. A group dashboard fed directly from each company's accounting system, replacing the hand-built monthly report.

Each step is measured against the old way before the next starts, and nothing is posted to the books without a person approving it.

How do the UAE PDPL and the National AI Strategy 2031 apply?

The UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) sets out how businesses may collect, use and share personal data, including having a lawful basis such as consent, limiting data to its purpose and respecting people's rights over their information. Healthcare providers carry additional obligations under UAE health data rules and the requirements of their emirate's health authority. Some free zones have their own data protection regimes, so we confirm which rules apply to you during the audit.

The UAE National Strategy for Artificial Intelligence 2031 has made AI a board-level topic for many owners and senior teams across the country. It sets the national direction, but it does not tell a business which process to change first: that still comes down to which workflow, measured how, owned by whom. Our AI adoption guide for UAE businesses walks through it step by step.

This is engineering practice, not legal advice. Confirm your obligations with your legal adviser, especially if you handle patient or financial data.

How to choose an AI consultant in Abu Dhabi

Questions worth asking any of the AI consultants Abu Dhabi businesses shortlist:

  • Where is your team based, and who will actually come on site?
  • Can you produce the documentation our procurement and IT teams need, and answer a security questionnaire yourselves?
  • Where will our data be stored and processed, and can the system run on our own cloud?
  • Can you show the agent working in Arabic on real past conversations from our business?
  • Will you connect to our existing accounting system, CRM and calendar, or do we have to switch?
  • Who supports the system after launch, and who owns the workflows and data if we part ways?

Red flags

  • A local address that turns out to be a meeting room, with no clarity on where the work is really done.
  • A fixed price quoted before anyone has looked at your enquiries, documents or systems.
  • WhatsApp tools that send bulk messages without opt-in, which put your number at risk.
  • Automations that post to the ledger or message patients with no human approval step.
