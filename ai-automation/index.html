What does an AI automation agency do?

An AI automation agency takes the repetitive work your team does between systems and makes software do it end to end. Not a single macro or a clever spreadsheet, but the whole chain: an invoice arrives by email, its details are read, checked against the purchase order, sent to the right person for approval and posted into TallyPrime or Zoho Books, with an audit trail at every step. The AI part is what lets this work on messy, real-world input such as scanned PDFs, free-text emails and supplier layouts that change without warning.

Crank Labs approaches business process automation as a consulting problem first and a software problem second. If the process is broken, automating it only makes the mistakes faster. So we map how the work actually flows, remove the steps that should not exist, and then automate what is left.

Which business processes can AI automate?

Most of the time we find in finance and operations teams sits in four kinds of work.

Invoices and bills

Read invoices from email, WhatsApp or a shared folder, extract supplier, amounts, tax and line items, match them to purchase orders or delivery notes, flag mismatches and post approved entries into TallyPrime, Zoho Books or your ERP. Accounting and bookkeeping firms use the same pattern across many client ledgers; our guide to AI bookkeeping automation for accounting firms covers that in detail.

Approvals

Purchase requests, discounts, refunds, leave and expense claims. Automation routes each request to the right approver based on value, department or branch, reminds them, records the decision and updates the system of record. The approver taps approve or reject on a phone instead of digging through email.

Reporting

Daily sales, collections, stock, job status and branch performance, pulled from the systems that hold them and delivered as a dashboard or a message every morning. No one spends Monday building last week's spreadsheet.

Data entry and system sync

Leads from forms into the CRM, orders from email into the ERP, customer details from one system into another. This is the least glamorous automation and often the one that saves the most hours, because it happens dozens of times a day.

Which processes should you automate first?

Do not start with the most painful process. Start with the one most likely to work and pay back quickly, so the team trusts the next one. Score each candidate from 1 to 5 on these six criteria.

CriterionScores 1Scores 5
VolumeA handful a monthMany every day
Rule clarityEvery case needs judgementClear rules a new hire could follow
Input consistencyHandwritten, varied, often incompleteDigital documents or forms with predictable fields
System accessNo API, no exports, desktop onlyTarget system accepts data through an API or import
Cost of an errorRegulatory, irreversible or customer-facingCaught and corrected easily
Process ownerNobody owns it end to endOne named person who will review and sign off

How to read the total out of 30:

  • 24 and above: automate now. This is your first workflow.
  • 16 to 23: automate with a human review step, or fix one weak criterion first, usually input consistency or system access.
  • Below 16: redesign the process before you automate it. Automating chaos produces faster chaos.

A typical example: a 30-person accounting firm serving small businesses in the UAE or Australia receives supplier invoices from dozens of clients in every format imaginable. Volume, rule clarity and system access score high; input consistency is middling and the cost of an error is real. That lands in the middle band, so the right build reads and codes every invoice automatically but holds each entry in a review queue until a bookkeeper approves it. Once the error rate on reviewed entries is low and stable, low-value entries from known suppliers can skip review.

How does human-in-the-loop automation work?

Every automation we build has a defined place where a person can step in. In practice that means:

  • Confidence thresholds. When the AI is unsure what it has read, the item goes to a person instead of being guessed.
  • An exception queue. Mismatches, new suppliers and missing fields land in one list with the source document attached, not in someone's inbox.
  • Approval before posting. For finance, a person approves before anything is sent or posted to the ledger. This is not optional in our builds.
  • A full audit trail. Who approved what, when, and what the automation proposed, so your auditor can follow it.
  • Learning from corrections. When a reviewer fixes an entry, that correction is used to improve the rules for the next one.

Over time the review step shrinks to the cases that genuinely need judgement. It should never disappear entirely for anything that moves money.

How does an automation project run?

  1. Diagnose. A free working session mapping how the work flows today, who touches it and where time and money leak.
  2. Score and choose. Run candidates through the table above and pick the first workflow.
  3. Redesign. Remove duplicate steps and agree the rules before any code is written.
  4. Build on real data. Use last month's actual invoices, requests or reports, not clean samples.
  5. Run in parallel. The automation and the team process the same items side by side until the results match.
  6. Go live with review. Switch over with the human approval step in place and the exception queue watched daily.
  7. Run and scale. Measure hours saved and errors caught, tune, train the team, then take on the next process.

A focused workflow such as invoice entry is typically live in two to four weeks. Larger multi-system programmes are phased so results show from the first month. When the change touches how a whole department works, it becomes business transformation rather than a single automation, and we run it that way.

Who maintains automations after launch?

Automations are not set and forget. Suppliers change invoice layouts, software vendors update their APIs, a new branch opens, tax rules change, a field gets renamed. An automation nobody watches fails quietly, and quiet failures in finance are the expensive kind. Whoever runs yours should provide:

  • Monitoring that alerts someone when a run fails or volumes drop unexpectedly.
  • A named person who fixes breaks, not a ticket that waits a week.
  • A regular review of the exception queue to spot new patterns worth automating.
  • Documentation of every rule, so your team understands what the automation does.

Red flags when choosing an AI automation agency

  • They quote before seeing a single real document from your process.
  • They propose replacing your accounting or CRM system to make the automation easier.
  • There is no human approval step for financial entries.
  • They rely on screen-scraping RPA when the system has a perfectly good API.
  • Nobody can tell you who fixes it when it breaks at month end.
  • Success is measured in "automations delivered" rather than hours saved or errors avoided.

Frequently asked questions

Can AI automation post entries directly into TallyPrime or Zoho Books?

Yes. Crank Labs builds automations that post into TallyPrime and Zoho Books, as well as popular CRMs and custom databases. For financial entries we keep a human approval step, so the automation prepares and checks each entry and a person approves it before it reaches the ledger. That keeps your books accurate and your auditor comfortable.

How long does it take to automate a business process?

A focused workflow such as invoice entry or a daily sales report is typically live in two to four weeks, including a period where the automation runs in parallel with your team. Larger programmes that touch several systems or departments are phased, so the first workflow delivers results in the first month while the next is built.

Do we need to change our software to automate our processes?

Usually not. Crank Labs builds around the tools you already use, including Microsoft 365, Gmail, TallyPrime, Zoho Books, popular CRMs and custom databases. Replacing core systems is rarely needed for automation, and an agency that insists on it before automating anything is often solving its own problem rather than yours.

What happens when the automation makes a mistake?

A well-built automation is designed to catch its own uncertainty. Items it is unsure about go to an exception queue for a person, and financial entries wait for approval before posting. Every action is logged, so mistakes are traceable and correctable. Corrections from reviewers are used to tighten the rules so the same mistake becomes less likely.

Is AI automation suitable for a business with 20 or 30 people?

Often it suits smaller teams best, because a few people carry a lot of manual work and every hour freed up matters. The scoring approach still applies: pick a frequent, rule-based process with clear inputs. If no process scores well yet, the first step is usually tidying the process itself rather than buying automation.

Who owns and maintains the automation after it goes live?

Crank Labs runs automations under a monthly managed plan that sits alongside the one-time build fee, covering monitoring, fixes when suppliers or software change, and tuning. Your team stays responsible for approving financial entries and owning the process. Every rule is documented so your people understand what the automation does and why.

Is our financial data secure during automation?

Automations run on infrastructure Crank Labs controls or on your own cloud, with access limited to what each workflow needs. A person approves finance entries before anything is posted. Data handling is planned with the UAE Personal Data Protection Law and India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 in mind, alongside any rules your sector applies.
