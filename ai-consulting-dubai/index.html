How do you design AI for WhatsApp-first customers?

For clinics, property brokerages, contractors and professional firms across the UAE, WhatsApp is the front desk. An agent built for that channel has to handle the way people really use it:

  • After-hours messages. Enquiries sent late in the evening or at the weekend need a useful reply then, not the next working morning.
  • Voice notes, photos and screenshots. A customer sends a photo of a ceiling, a screenshot of a ring from Instagram or a voice note in Arabic. The agent has to transcribe or read these, or pass them to a person quickly.
  • Comparison shopping. The business that replies clearly first often wins the booking. Speed matters more than polish.
  • Personal phone sprawl. Many businesses run sales from staff mobiles, so conversations and customer history leave when the staff member does. Moving onto one official number fixes that.
  • Platform rules. The WhatsApp Business Platform requires opt-in for business-initiated messages and approved templates outside the customer service window. Unofficial bulk tools that ignore this put your number at risk.

How should an agent handle Arabic, English and mixed-language enquiries?

A Dubai business can receive messages in English, Arabic, Hindi, Urdu, Russian and more in the same afternoon. Many people mix languages in one message, or write Arabic in Latin letters with numbers standing in for some sounds. Good multilingual design is mostly about rules, not just translation.

SituationWhat the agent should do
Message in ArabicReply in Arabic using approved answers that a fluent Arabic speaker on your team has checked, not raw machine translation
Arabic in Latin script, or Arabic and English mixedRecognise it, reply in the language the customer seems most comfortable in, and keep the tone natural
A language the business does not supportReply briefly in English, offer the supported options and flag the conversation for staff
Customer switches language mid-conversationFollow the switch rather than forcing the original language
Pricing disputes, complaints or anything clinicalHand over to a named person with the conversation summarised, whatever the language

We test every agent on real past conversations in each supported language before launch. Staff see a summary in English, so the front desk can follow up without reading every language.

A worked example: a Dubai smart-home contractor

A 30-person smart-home and electrical contractor in Dubai typically gets enquiries from Instagram, Google and referrals, almost all of which end up on WhatsApp, in English and Arabic. Site surveys are booked by phone. The surveyor sends photos and notes to a group chat, someone builds the quote in a spreadsheet days later, and invoices are raised in Zoho Books when a project manager remembers to say a milestone is done.

A sensible sequence for that business:

  1. One official WhatsApp number with an agent that qualifies the job (villa or apartment, new build or retrofit, which emirate, scope, timing) and books the survey straight into the surveyor's calendar. Typically live in two to four weeks.
  2. A survey form on the surveyor's phone that feeds a quote builder, so a draft quote is ready the same day.
  3. Project milestones that trigger invoice drafts in Zoho Books through workflow automation, approved by the accounts team before they go out.

Each step is measured against the old way before the next one starts. The contractor keeps its existing tools, and nobody has to learn a new accounting system.

How do you handle UAE PDPL and healthcare data?

The UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) sets rules on how businesses collect, use and share personal data, including having a lawful basis such as consent, limiting data to its purpose and respecting people's rights over their information. Businesses registered in the DIFC and ADGM free zones fall under those zones' own data protection regimes. Healthcare providers carry additional obligations under UAE health data rules and the requirements of their emirate's health authority.

In practice, every system we build follows these principles:

  • Agents run on infrastructure Crank Labs controls or on your own cloud.
  • Each workflow can reach only the data it needs. A booking agent sees the calendar, not the full patient record.
  • For healthcare and finance, a person approves before anything is sent or posted.
  • Marketing and reactivation messages go only to contacts who have opted in, and opt-outs are honoured immediately.
  • Conversations and actions are logged, so you can answer questions about what happened and when.

This is engineering practice, not legal advice. Confirm your obligations with your legal adviser, especially if you handle patient data.
