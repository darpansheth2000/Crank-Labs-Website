The hard part is rarely the language model. It is deciding what the chatbot may say, where its answers come from, which systems it can read or update, and exactly when it stops and passes the conversation to a person.

Which channels can an AI chatbot in Dubai work on?

Customers in the UAE rarely stay on one channel. A sensible build shares one knowledge base and one set of rules across all of them, so every channel gives the same answer.

  • Website chat. The usual starting point: answers questions while the visitor is on the page and captures contact details before they leave.
  • WhatsApp. For many UAE businesses this is the main front door. It runs on the official WhatsApp Business Platform with its own opt-in and template rules, which we cover on our WhatsApp chatbot page.
  • Instagram direct messages. Common where enquiries start from a post or an advert. Same content, with serious leads passed to sales.
  • Email. Drafts replies to incoming enquiries from approved content, sent directly or queued for approval.
  • Voice. Missed calls and after-hours lines can be handled by an AI voice agent that uses the same knowledge and booking rules.

Start with the channel where enquiries wait longest today, then add others once the first is trusted.

How should an Arabic chatbot handle Arabic and English?

An Arabic chatbot for a Dubai audience is not an English chatbot with translation bolted on. Customers write in Modern Standard Arabic, in Gulf and other dialects, in Arabic typed with Latin letters and numbers, and in a mix of Arabic and English within one message. Product and brand names often stay in English. Good design handles this with clear rules:

  • Reply in the language the customer is using, and follow them if they switch mid-conversation.
  • Keep approved Arabic wording for prices, policies and anything with legal weight, rather than translating English answers on the fly.
  • Agree a glossary of names and terms that should never be translated.
  • Give staff an English summary of every Arabic conversation, so the whole team can follow up.
  • Have someone fluent on your team review the Arabic answers during testing, before launch.

How do you stop an AI chatbot from making things up?

A chatbot that is confidently wrong does more damage than no chatbot. Three design choices keep answers accurate.

Ground every answer in your own documents and data

The chatbot answers from a defined set of sources: your web pages, service descriptions, price lists, policies, brochures and FAQs, plus live data where it is needed, such as availability or order status. If the answer is not in those sources, it says so and offers a person.

Set guardrails in writing

  • Topics it must never advise on, such as medical, legal or financial questions beyond your published information.
  • No discounts, commitments or prices that are not in the approved sources.
  • Polite refusal for off-topic requests and for attempts to make it ignore its instructions.
  • Clear disclosure that the customer is talking to an AI assistant.
  • Every conversation logged for review.

Decide when to hand over to a human

Handover rules are part of the design, not an afterthought. Typical triggers are a request for a person, a complaint or dispute, a question the sources cannot answer, a high-value lead ready to buy, or anything sensitive. The handover passes a short summary and the full conversation to the right person or branch, so the customer never has to repeat themselves.

How does the UAE PDPL affect an AI chatbot?

A chatbot collects personal data from the first message: names, phone numbers, email addresses and often details of what the customer wants. The UAE Personal Data Protection Law (Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021) sets rules on lawful processing, transparency, security and people's rights over their data. Businesses in DIFC or ADGM fall under those free zones' own data protection rules. In practice, a well-built chatbot:

  • Links to your privacy notice.
  • Collects only what the next step needs.
  • Runs on infrastructure Crank Labs controls or on your own cloud, with access limited to what each workflow needs.
  • Keeps sensitive details, such as health information, with staff rather than the chatbot.
  • Uses a human approval step before anything is sent or posted in finance and healthcare workflows.

Frequently asked questions

Can the chatbot be trained on our existing website and documents?

Yes. Your web pages, brochures, policies, price lists and internal FAQs become the chatbot's sources, and it answers only from them. Part of the build is cleaning that content up, because outdated or contradictory documents produce inconsistent answers. Where information changes often, such as availability or stock, the chatbot reads it live from your system instead of from a document, so customers see current details.

What happens when our prices or policies change?

You update the source content, or tell us and we update it, and the chatbot's answers follow. Because answers come from versioned sources rather than being written into the chatbot itself, a change does not need a rebuild. Content updates and tuning are part of the monthly managed plan, and we recommend a named owner on your side for each topic so changes are approved before they go live.

Do we need a separate chatbot for each channel?

No. A well-designed build uses one knowledge base, one set of rules and one handover process across website chat, WhatsApp, Instagram and email, with small adjustments for each channel's format and rules. That keeps answers consistent wherever the customer asks, and means you update content once. Most businesses launch on one channel first and add the others once answers are trusted.

Can you improve a chatbot we already have?

Often, yes. We start by reviewing its conversation logs to see where it fails: questions it cannot answer, wrong answers, poor Arabic or missed handovers. Sometimes the fix is better content and rules on the existing platform. Sometimes a rebuild is the cheaper route, particularly if the current bot is menu-based and cannot handle free text. You get a plain recommendation after the review.

Which language model does Crank Labs use for chatbots?

We choose the model for each build based on the languages involved, the type of questions, the volume of conversations and your data rules, rather than tying every client to one provider. The model matters less than most buyers expect. Accuracy depends far more on the quality of your source content, the guardrails, the handover rules and testing on your real past enquiries.

Will customers know they are talking to an AI?

They should, and we design for it. The chatbot introduces itself as an AI assistant, links to your privacy notice and offers a person whenever the customer asks. Being open about it builds trust and fits the transparency principles in the UAE Personal Data Protection Law. Customers mostly care that they get a fast, accurate answer and can reach a human when they need one.
