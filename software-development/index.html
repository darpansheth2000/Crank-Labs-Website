What is custom software development, and when is it worth it?

Custom software development means building the web apps, portals, dashboards and integrations your business needs around the way your team actually works, instead of reshaping the team to fit a product built for someone else. It is worth it when the work that sets you apart, or the work that joins your systems together, is being held together by spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups and people retyping data.

Crank Labs does full stack software development as part of a wider consulting practice. That matters because most failed software projects were built to a feature list nobody questioned. We start with the process, agree what should change, and only then decide what to build. Off-the-shelf tools stay where they do the job well.

Should you build or buy software?

Our rule of thumb: buy for the commodity, build for the glue and the edge. Accounting, payroll and email are solved problems; buy them. The workflow that is specific to how you quote, schedule, produce or serve customers, and the connections between your systems, are where custom work pays.

What kinds of software do businesses build with us?

Customer, dealer and supplier portals

A secure place where outside parties see order status, documents, invoices and next steps, and submit requests without a phone call. For a kitchen and interior manufacturer, that might mean customers following their order from design sign-off through production to installation, with photos and dates at each stage.

Business dashboards

One screen showing sales, collections, production, stock or job status, built on the data already sitting in Tally, Zoho, your CRM and your spreadsheets. A good dashboard answers the five questions the owner asks every week, not fifty. If a number on it cannot be traced back to its source, nobody will trust it.

Internal tools

Job cards, inspection checklists, quoting tools, approval flows and scheduling boards that replace the spreadsheet everybody is afraid to edit. These are often small, used daily and save more time than any large system.

System integration

Connecting what you already have: the website form to the CRM, the CRM to accounting, the WhatsApp Business Platform to the calendar, Microsoft 365 or Gmail to your custom database. Integration work is unglamorous and usually where the quickest wins are. Where the integration also involves reading documents or making decisions, it overlaps with our AI automation service.

Why does consulting-led scoping matter?

The expensive mistakes in software happen before any code is written. A team builds exactly what was asked for, and it turns out the request was a workaround for a process that should have been changed. Consulting-led scoping prevents that. Before a build, we produce:

  • A process map of how the work flows today, who touches it and where it waits.
  • The target process, agreed with the people who will use it, not just the person signing off.
  • A data map showing which system holds the truth for each piece of information.
  • Users and permissions, including what each role can see and change.
  • A first release small enough to ship in weeks and useful enough that people choose to use it.
  • A measure of success in hours saved, errors avoided, faster collections or fewer status calls.

This is the core idea of full stack consulting: the people who diagnose the problem are the people who build and roll out the fix. For a wider review before choosing what to build, start with AI consulting and strategy.

How is custom software delivered in phases?

We do not believe in a long silent build followed by a big launch. Delivery is phased so results show from the first month.

  1. Diagnose. A free working session mapping how leads, operations and finance flow and where time and money leak.
  2. First release. One workflow, one team, real data, live in weeks. Enough to prove the approach and expose the questions nobody thought to ask.
  3. Extend. Add the next workflows, users and integrations in short cycles, each one reviewed with the people using it.
  4. Roll out. Train the team, migrate remaining data, retire the old spreadsheets, and make sure people actually switch.
  5. Run and scale. Measure, fix, improve, then take on the next function once the first change pays for itself.

A typical example: a 150-person manufacturer of specialist vehicles in India tracks each build across engineering, purchasing, the shop floor and quality using spreadsheets and daily calls. A first release gives the production manager a live board of every vehicle and its current stage, fed by simple updates from supervisors on their phones. Phase two links purchase orders and material shortages to each build. Phase three adds the quality checklist and a dashboard for the owner. Each phase is useful on its own, so value does not wait for the whole system.

Who owns the code and the data?

Settle this in writing before any build starts, whoever you hire. The questions that matter:

  • Code. Who owns the source code, and can you get a full copy whenever you ask?
  • Data. Your business data should always be yours, exportable in a standard format without a fee or a fight.
  • Hosting. Will it run on infrastructure the developer controls or on your own cloud account? Both can be sensible; know which.
  • Accounts. Domains, cloud accounts and third-party subscriptions should be in your company's name, not a developer's personal one.
  • Documentation. Enough that another competent team could take over if they had to.
  • Access. Each user and integration should have only the permissions it needs, and access should be removable in minutes when someone leaves.

Crank Labs runs systems either on infrastructure we control or on the client's cloud, with access limited to what each workflow needs, and we build around your existing tools so your data stays in the systems you already rely on.

What should you ask a custom software development company?

  • Who will I speak to each week, and are they the people building it?
  • What will be live and in use by the end of the first month?
  • How will you work with our existing systems instead of replacing them?
  • How do you handle changes to scope once we see the first release?
  • Who fixes problems after launch, and how quickly?
  • Can I see the software running at each stage, not just at the end?

Red flags: a fixed price for a large system before anyone has mapped the process, no working software until the final month, a proposal to replace tools that work fine, vague answers on code and data ownership, and a sales team you never hear from again once the developers start.

Frequently asked questions

How long does custom software development take?

It depends on scope, but we do not run long silent builds. A focused first release, such as a dashboard on existing data or a single integration, can be live within weeks. Larger multi-system builds are phased, with something useful in your team's hands from the first month and further workflows added in short cycles after that.

Is custom software more expensive than buying a SaaS product?

Not always. For standard needs such as accounting or payroll, buying is almost always cheaper. For workflows specific to your business, or for joining tools that do not talk to each other, a focused custom build can cost less over time than stacking subscriptions and manual work. Crank Labs prices builds as a one-time fee plus a monthly managed plan, quoted after the free audit.

Will you replace our existing systems?

Usually not. Crank Labs builds around the tools you already rely on, such as TallyPrime, Zoho Books, Microsoft 365, Gmail, popular CRMs and custom databases. Replacing a core system is a big decision with real risk, so we only suggest it when the existing system is genuinely blocking the business and cannot be connected.

What technology do you use for web app development?

We use modern, widely supported web technologies so that the software is maintainable and another team could pick it up if needed. The specific choice depends on where it will run, who maintains it and what it connects to. For most business owners the right question is not the framework but who owns the code, the data and the hosting.

Can you work with our in-house IT team?

Yes. Many businesses have someone who manages systems, accounts or a website. We agree who owns which part, share access through proper accounts rather than shared passwords, and document what we build so your team understands it. Training the people who will run and use the software is part of the rollout.

Do you build mobile apps?

We build web apps designed to work well on phones, and for most internal tools and portals that is the better choice: it avoids app store approvals and keeps one version for every device. Supervisors updating job status on the shop floor or staff approving requests on the move can do it from a browser. A native app makes sense only when there is a clear reason.
