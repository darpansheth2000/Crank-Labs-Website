AI for manufacturers

AI for manufacturing companies that build to order

For small and mid-sized manufacturers, the biggest gains sit around the machines, not on them: enquiries, quotes, planning, materials, quality, dispatch and customer updates. We build the systems that join those up, around the ERP or TallyPrime you already run.

In short

AI for manufacturing companies, especially build-to-order and mid-sized ones, works best on the information around production: replying to enquiries and drafting quotes, replacing spreadsheet plans with a live production board, digital quality checks, material requests, dispatch documents and WhatsApp updates for dealers and customers. It should connect to your existing ERP or TallyPrime rather than replace it, start with one workflow that is live in weeks, and add AI features once orders are captured digitally.

What does AI for manufacturing companies actually do?

AI for manufacturing companies is less about robots and more about the messages, spreadsheets and paperwork that surround production. For a build-to-order or mid-sized manufacturer, the useful work is practical: reply to enquiries and draft quotations faster, put every order on a live production board instead of a spreadsheet, capture quality checks on the shop floor, raise material requests before a job stalls, produce dispatch documents from the order record and keep dealers and customers updated on WhatsApp without anyone ringing round. AI handles the open-ended parts, such as reading a customer's message or summarising inspector notes. Software and plain automation do the rest.

This page is for owners and plant heads of specialist vehicle builders, kitchen and interior makers, corporate gifting manufacturers, fabricators and similar businesses, where every order is a little different and the real bottleneck is information rather than machine time. Why do AI in manufacturing projects stall in smaller factories?

Most writing on AI in manufacturing assumes a high-volume line full of sensors. A 60-person fabrication shop or a kitchen maker producing one-off orders does not look like that. Its problems sit upstream and downstream of the machines:

  • Enquiries arrive on WhatsApp, email and phone, and every quote depends on one estimator who is always busy.
  • The production plan is a spreadsheet that one person updates, so it is out of date by mid-morning.
  • Material shortages are discovered when the job reaches the stage that needs them.
  • Quality checks live on paper, so nobody can pull the full record of one order without a search.
  • Dealers and customers chase status by phone, and the answer depends on who picks up.
  • The owner hears about problems in the weekly meeting, once they have cost money.

No AI tool fixes these on its own. The fix is one live record per order, with automation and AI on top.

How should enquiry and quotation work in a build-to-order business?

The quote is where build-to-order revenue is won or lost, and where senior people spend hours retyping. Split the job in two.

Capture the enquiry properly the first time

An AI agent on WhatsApp and email replies in seconds, asks the questions your estimator would ask (dimensions, specification, quantity, deadline), collects drawings and photos, and opens the enquiry in your CRM or order system. Dealer requests arrive complete, not as a voice note and a blurry photo.

Draft the quote, let a person approve it

The quotation is drafted from your rate card, standard options and similar past jobs, then sent to the estimator to check. In the builds we design, nothing priced goes to a customer without that approval. Quotes that go quiet trigger a reminder for the salesperson, and any follow-up message the business starts uses approved templates and the customer's opt-in, as the WhatsApp Business Platform requires.

Production planning software or a spreadsheet: what changes with a live board?

A spreadsheet plan works until there are more orders than one planner can hold in their head. After that it is a record of what was planned on Monday. Production planning software built as a live board behaves differently because the floor updates it, not the planner.

Where does digital quality control fit?

Digital quality control pays off most when it is joined to the rest of the order. We cover the inspection side in depth, from per-unit checklists to camera inspection, in our guide to AI in manufacturing quality control. Here the point is the joins:

  • Checklists come from the order's specification, so the board, the QC form and the dispatch pack all describe the same product.
  • An open defect holds the order on the board, so nothing ships with an unresolved issue unless a named person releases it.
  • Defects tagged to a supplier feed back into purchasing, so repeat problems are visible when the next order is placed.
  • The QC record drops into the dispatch documents without retyping.

How do material requests, purchase and dispatch get automated?

Material requests and purchase

When an order is confirmed, its bill of materials can generate material requests against stock. Shortfalls become purchase requests with an approval step, and approved requests become draft purchase orders for the buyer to send. When the supplier's invoice arrives by email, workflow automation reads it, matches it to the purchase order and goods received, and posts it into TallyPrime or Zoho Books once a person approves.

Dispatch and documentation

Packing lists, delivery notes, QC packs, warranty cards and invoice drafts can all be produced from the order record, because the record already holds the specification, the checks and the customer details. A manager reviews and signs off. Nobody retypes.

How do you keep dealers and customers updated on WhatsApp?

Most status calls can be answered from the production board. Two pieces make that work:

  1. Milestone updates. When an order reaches an agreed stage, such as materials received, in production, QC passed or dispatched, the customer or dealer receives an approved WhatsApp template message. Opt-in is captured at order confirmation.
  2. Status on demand. When a dealer asks about an order, the agent reads the record and replies with the current stage and expected date. Anything outside its rules, such as a change request or a complaint, goes to a named person with the conversation attached.

What should an owner dashboard show?

  • Enquiries, quotes sent and quotes waiting for follow-up.
  • Orders by stage, and those at risk of missing their date.
  • Orders blocked by materials, and purchase orders awaiting approval.
  • Open and repeat defects by product line.
  • Orders ready to dispatch and invoices not yet raised.
  • A short daily summary in plain language, written by AI from the same records and sent on WhatsApp or email.

Can AI work with our existing ERP or Tally?

Yes, and in most cases it should. Manufacturing ERP integration does not mean replacing the ERP: your ERP or TallyPrime stays the system of record for accounts, stock valuation and statutory documents. The new layer handles what the ERP is clumsy at: shop-floor updates, the live board, WhatsApp conversations and the owner dashboard.

  • Orders flow in from the ERP or accounting system when confirmed, so the board and QC forms never need retyping.
  • Status and documents flow back: completed stages, QC records, goods received and dispatch details, with invoices posted after approval.
  • Use the API where there is one. Where an older system has none, a scheduled export, a shared database or a small connector usually works.
  • One owner per field. Each piece of data belongs to one system, so the two never disagree.

Replacing an ERP is a large, risky project that rarely fixes the floor. Our custom software development team builds the board, portals and connectors.

What does a phased plan look like?

  1. Diagnose. A free working session maps how an order moves from enquiry to cash and where time leaks.
  2. First workflow live. Usually the enquiry agent or a live board for one product line. A focused workflow like this is typically live in two to four weeks.
  3. Connect the record. Link the board to the ERP or TallyPrime, add digital QC and material requests for that line, and run it alongside the old spreadsheet until supervisors trust it.
  4. Add updates and documents. WhatsApp updates and dispatch paperwork come next, because they rely on an accurate board.
  5. Layer in AI. Delay flags, defect patterns and daily summaries arrive once there are a few months of structured records.
  6. Scale. Take on the next product line or function once the first change has paid for itself.

Larger multi-system builds follow the same phasing, so results show from the first month.

What does this look like for a kitchen and interior manufacturer?

Consider a generic kitchen and interior manufacturer with around 100 people, one factory, two showrooms and a network of dealers and designers. Each kitchen is measured, designed, quoted, revised, then cut, edged, assembled, finished, packed and installed. Today quotes queue with two designers, the plan is a shared spreadsheet and hardware shortages surface at assembly.

A typical phased build starts with the enquiry: a WhatsApp agent collects room dimensions, photos, style and timeline, books a measurement visit and opens the record. Each confirmed order then becomes a card on a live board, with stages from cutting to installation and material status taken from the bill of materials, so board and hardware shortages raise purchase requests at confirmation rather than at assembly. A photo packing checklist travels with each delivery. Customers receive template updates at confirmation, production start, dispatch and installation. TallyPrime keeps the accounts, and invoice drafts are posted to it for approval.

What should you ask a partner before you start?

  • How does the system handle an order that changes halfway through?
  • Will you walk the floor and design with supervisors and inspectors, not only management?
  • How does it connect to our ERP or TallyPrime, and which system owns each piece of data?
  • Does shop-floor capture work offline and in the languages our team speaks?
  • What goes live first, and how will we measure whether it worked?
  • Who maintains it when products, rates or suppliers change?

Red flags

  • A proposal that starts by replacing your ERP.
  • Camera inspection or predictive maintenance pitched before your orders are even digital.
  • A demo built on someone else's data, with no plan for your rate card, bills of materials or checklists.
  • Quotes sent to customers automatically with no approval step.
  • Unofficial WhatsApp tools instead of the WhatsApp Business Platform.
  • A fixed price before anyone has seen how an order moves through your floor.

Frequently asked questions

Is AI worth it for a small manufacturer with under 50 people?

Often yes, if you start with the workflow that costs the most time, such as enquiry response, quotation or order status calls. AI for small manufacturers works best as one focused build connected to the tools you already use, not a factory-wide programme. Smaller teams usually feel the difference quickly because the same few people handle sales, planning and dispatch.

Do we need sensors or IoT on our machines to use AI?

No. For build-to-order and mid-sized manufacturers, most of the value sits in orders, quotes, plans, inspections and documents, which need people entering updates on a phone or tablet rather than sensors. Machine data can be added later if a specific problem, such as unplanned downtime on one critical machine, justifies it. Start with the information your team already handles every day.

Can a WhatsApp agent send prices to our customers?

It can share approved standard prices or catalogues, but for build-to-order work we recommend that the agent collects the requirement and drafts the quote, and a person approves it before it is sent. Custom specifications, discounts and delivery terms carry real commercial risk, so an approval step keeps your estimator in control while removing the retyping.

What if our floor team is not comfortable with technology?

Design for them, not for the office. Shop-floor screens should mirror the sheets people already fill in, use large buttons and photos rather than typing, work offline and support the languages the team speaks. Running the new board alongside the old sheet for a short period, and fixing what supervisors find awkward, is what earns adoption.

How long does it take to go live?

A focused first workflow, such as WhatsApp enquiry capture or a live board for one product line, is typically live in two to four weeks. Builds that connect several functions and an ERP are phased, so the first part goes live early and results show from the first month while the rest is built and connected.

Where is our production and customer data stored?

Systems run on infrastructure Crank Labs controls or on your own cloud, with access limited to what each workflow needs and every action logged. Where finance is involved, such as posting supplier invoices into TallyPrime or Zoho Books, a person approves before anything is posted. Consent and retention for customer data are planned with India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and the UAE Personal Data Protection Law in mind.

Can AI plan our production automatically?

It can propose a plan, but it should not run the floor unsupervised. With a live board and a few months of stage history, AI can flag jobs likely to run late and suggest a re-sequenced week based on due dates, capacity and material status. The planner accepts or changes it, because they know about absences, rush orders and customer priorities the system cannot see.
