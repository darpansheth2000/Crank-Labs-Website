Dubai and Mumbai do not change their clocks, so the gap with the UK shifts twice a year. During GMT, 9am in London is 1pm in Dubai and 2.30pm in Mumbai. A worked example: a 35-person UK accountancy practice

A 35-person accountancy practice typically has a few partners, a bookkeeping team, payroll and a mix of limited company, sole trader and landlord clients. Records arrive by email as PDFs, receipt photos and bank statement downloads. Bookkeeping lives in Xero or QuickBooks Online. New enquiries come through the website form and the main phone line, and the team loses hours every quarter chasing clients for missing records before VAT returns are due.

HMRC's Making Tax Digital programme adds to the load. VAT-registered businesses must keep digital records and submit returns through compatible software, and the regime is being extended to income tax for many sole traders and landlords. For a practice, that means more digital bookkeeping and more chasing for the same team.

A sensible sequence for that practice:

  1. Document intake. A shared Microsoft 365 mailbox where clients send records. An automation reads each attachment, identifies the client, extracts the key fields and posts a draft entry into Xero or QuickBooks Online for a bookkeeper to approve. Typically live in two to four weeks.
  2. Records chasing. Before each VAT quarter, clients with missing records get a clear email listing exactly what is outstanding, with follow-ups on a schedule the partners approve. Non-responders become a call task for their manager.
  3. New enquiries. An email agent and an out-of-hours voice agent that answer common questions about services, take the prospect's details, check fit and book a discovery call with the right partner.
  4. Partner dashboard. One view of work in progress, outstanding records and deadlines, instead of a hand-updated spreadsheet.

Each step is measured against the old way, in hours spent and days to complete a quarter's records, before the next starts. Nothing is filed with HMRC by the system, and no entry is posted without a person approving it. How do you handle UK GDPR when the team is outside the UK?

UK GDPR, together with the Data Protection Act 2018, governs how UK businesses handle personal data, and the Information Commissioner's Office is the regulator. For an AI project the points that matter most are high level but important:

  • Lawful basis. Every workflow that touches personal data needs a lawful basis, and health data handled by clinics is special category data with extra conditions. We document what data each workflow uses and why.
  • Data processing agreement. Where Crank Labs processes personal data on your behalf, UK GDPR requires a written contract between controller and processor. We put one in place before any live data is touched.
  • International transfers. Our team works from the UAE and India, so we treat our access to UK personal data as a restricted transfer. That needs an appropriate safeguard, such as the ICO's International Data Transfer Agreement or the UK Addendum to the EU standard contractual clauses, along with a transfer risk assessment.
  • Where the data lives. We can build on your own cloud account in a UK or EU region, so records stay where you choose, and our access is limited, logged and removable at any time.
  • Marketing rules. Email and phone marketing also falls under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, so any outbound or reactivation campaign is built around consent and opt-out from the start.

Finance and healthcare workflows also keep a human approval step before anything is sent or posted. This is a summary of engineering practice, not legal advice. Confirm your obligations with your own data protection adviser or solicitor.

How to choose an AI automation agency in the UK

Questions that separate firms that build working systems from firms that sell slide decks:

  • Where is your team based, and which hours can we actually get you on a call?
  • Will you sign a data processing agreement, and how do you cover international transfers if your team is outside the UK?
  • Can the system run in our own Microsoft 365 tenancy or cloud account, in a UK or EU region?
  • Will you connect to our existing accounting software and CRM, or do we have to switch?
  • Who supports the system after launch, and how quickly do they respond when something breaks?
  • Who owns the workflows, prompts and data if we part ways?

Red flags

  • A UK address that turns out to be a virtual office, with no clarity on where the work is really done.
  • A price quoted before anyone has looked at your enquiries, documents or systems.
  • A demo built on sample data that never touches your real inbox or accounting software.
  • Automations that post to the ledger or email clients with no human approval step.
